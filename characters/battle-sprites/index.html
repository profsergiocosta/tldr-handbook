    Battle sprites and icons¶

    Right now Berto fights as Susie: you registered him in the previous chapter, and everything you did not set in party_m_berto came from the base party_m, which holds Susie's battle sprites. This chapter gives him his own, plus his own menu icons.

    It is the biggest art job in Part 5, so it is done one piece at a time: first you see him fight with the borrowed sprites, then you replace them in the order the battle needs them, testing after each.

    How the engine finds a battle sprite¶

    Overworld sprites are found by name (spr_berto_down). Battle sprites are not: they are listed, directly as assets, in a struct called battle_sprites inside the party member. Here is Noelle's, trimmed (scripts/party_init/party_init.gml, in party_m_noelle):

    battle_sprites = {
    idle:       spr_bnoelle_idle,
    hurt:       spr_bnoelle_hurt,
    attack:     [spr_bnoelle_attack, true],
    actend:     [spr_bnoelle_actend, "idle", 1],
    // ...
}

    Each entry is one of three shapes:

    Shape Meaning
    spr_x Just play this sprite (it loops).
    [spr_x, true] Play once and freeze on the last frame.
    [spr_x, "idle", 1] Play once, then switch to another entry (here idle) at the given speed.

    This is decided when the animation ends (objects/o_actor/Other_7.gml).

    The keys, and when the battle asks for each:

    Key When it is used
    idle Standing in battle. Also used to measure his height for centring (party_getbattleheight).
    hurt When he takes a hit.
    defeat When his HP is zero or below.
    attack During the attack (the FIGHT timing bar), and in the battle intro.
    attackready Once FIGHT has been chosen for him, while he waits for the attack to start.
    attack_eff The slash effect shown on the enemy when the attack lands.
    actready, act, actend ACT chosen for him, performing the act, returning to idle.
    spellready, spell A spell chosen, casting.
    itemready, itemuse An item chosen, using it.
    defend The Defend button.
    spare Sparing an enemy.
    victory When the fight is won.
    intro, introb The battle intro, depending on s_battle_intro (below).

    What happens if an entry is missing¶

    For most keys, nothing bad: when the battle asks for a sprite the party member does not have, the actor keeps whatever it was showing (enc_party_set_battle_sprite, scripts/enc_scripts/enc_scripts.gml). So a character without an act sprite simply does not change pose when you ACT.

    Three keys are different, because the engine reads them directly: idle, hurt and defeat. Treat those, plus attack and attack_eff, as required. I have not run a battle with them deliberately missing, so this is read from the code, not tested.

    Every sprite you name must exist

    The struct refers to sprites as assets, not as text. If you write spr_bberto_idle and that sprite does not exist in the Asset Browser, the game does not show a placeholder: it fails. Create the sprite first, then add it to the struct.

    What s_battle_intro does¶

    s_battle_intro in the constructor chooses how the battle begins (objects/o_enc_anim/Alarm_1.gml and Alarm_2.gml):

    Value Behaviour
    0 Plays your intro sprite as the full intro.
    1 No intro sprite; he goes straight to the attack pose. This is the base default.
    2 Uses your introb sprite during the intro, then attack.

    Leave it at 1. That way you need neither intro nor introb.

    Step 1 — See him fight as Susie¶

    Before drawing, confirm the pipeline works. Open room_test_characters, add Berto to the party with Tab + P (right arrow four times, Enter), then start a fight with Tab + E (Encounter Select) and pick any encounter.

    Berto fights, with Susie's sprites and Susie's placeholder icon. (If the game crashes the first time he attacks, with weapon_element in the error, he has no weapon: see the danger box in Your first party member, Step 2.) Use Tab + W to leave the fight. If this step fails, fix it before drawing anything.

    Step 2 — Replace idle, hurt and defeat¶

    These three come first because the engine reads them directly.

    What to draw¶

    Look at what the engine's own characters use, so yours are consistent:

    Sprite Size (Noelle) Frames Fps Origin
    spr_bnoelle_idle 26 × 46 5 6 14, 46
    spr_bnoelle_hurt 32 × 46 1 0 15, 46
    spr_bnoelle_defeat 23 × 46 1 0 11, 46

    Two rules to copy:

    • The origin is at the feet, as in the overworld (the bottom row of the sprite, on the horizontal centre of the body). Keep it in the same place for all of a character's battle sprites: if idle has the origin at (14, 46) and attack at (30, 20), Berto jumps every time the sprite changes. A pixel of difference, like Noelle's hurt, is fine.
    • Different poses may be different widths (Noelle's idle is 26 wide, her act is 51 wide), but the height stays the same (46) so the feet stay on the ground.

    Animated sprites use the Fps of the engine's own: about 6 for idle. hurt and defeat are a single frame, so Fps does not matter for them.

    Wire them in¶

    1. Create spr_bberto_idle, spr_bberto_hurt and spr_bberto_defeat, with the origin at the feet.

    2. In party_m_berto (your script), add below s_name:

      s_battle_intro = 1

battle_sprites.idle   = spr_bberto_idle
battle_sprites.hurt   = spr_bberto_hurt
battle_sprites.defeat = spr_bberto_defeat

      This only works because each party member gets its own copy of the base battle_sprites struct. You are replacing three entries in Berto's copy; the other entries are still Susie's, and Susie is not affected.

    3. Run, add Berto, start a fight. In the fight, Berto stands as himself. Take a hit on purpose (let an enemy attack) to see hurt, and let his HP reach zero to see defeat.

    A mixed set will look off

    While the other entries are still Susie's, he changes from his sprite to hers when he attacks, and the origins will not line up. That is expected. The next steps remove it.

    Step 3 — The attack¶

    Now the fight itself: attackready (FIGHT highlighted), attack (the action) and attack_eff (the slash on the enemy).

    Sprite Size (Noelle) Frames Fps Origin
    spr_bnoelle_attackready 23 × 46 1 0 11, 46
    spr_bnoelle_attack 37 × 46 5 10 14, 46
    spr_bnoelle_attackeff 44 × 45 4 15 22, 22

    attack_eff is the exception to the feet rule: it is an effect, so its origin is its centre (22, 22 on a 44 × 45 sprite), because it is drawn on top of the enemy.

    battle_sprites.attackready = spr_bberto_attackready
battle_sprites.attack      = [spr_bberto_attack, true]
battle_sprites.attack_eff  = spr_bberto_attackeff

    attack is [sprite, true]: it plays once and freezes on its last frame, which is how the engine holds the pose at the end of the swing.

    Test: in a fight, choose FIGHT for Berto (he takes his ready pose) and attack.

    Step 4 — The rest¶

    These are optional (see What happens if an entry is missing), and each can be added later, one at a time. A character without them just does not change pose for that action.

    Entry Suggested shape
    act [spr_bberto_act, true]
    actready spr_bberto_actready
    actend [spr_bberto_actend, "idle", 1]
    spell / spellready [spr_bberto_spell, "idle", 1] / spr_bberto_spellready
    itemuse / itemready [spr_bberto_item, "idle", 1] / spr_bberto_itemready
    defend [spr_bberto_defend, true]
    spare [spr_bberto_act, "idle", 1] (Noelle and Susie reuse their act)
    victory [spr_bberto_victory, true]

    These shapes come from Noelle's and Susie's structs. defend is a plain sprite for Noelle and [sprite, true] for Susie; either works.

    When you have everything, you can stop patching the base struct and write the whole struct in the constructor instead, so that nothing of Susie's is left:

    battle_sprites = {
    act:         [spr_bberto_act, true],
    actready:    spr_bberto_actready,
    actend:      [spr_bberto_actend, "idle", 1],
    attack:      [spr_bberto_attack, true],
    attackready: spr_bberto_attackready,
    defeat:      spr_bberto_defeat,
    defend:      [spr_bberto_defend, true],
    hurt:        spr_bberto_hurt,
    idle:        spr_bberto_idle,
    intro:       spr_bberto_idle,   // unused while s_battle_intro = 1
    introb:      spr_bberto_idle,   // unused while s_battle_intro = 1
    itemuse:     [spr_bberto_item, "idle", 1],
    itemready:   spr_bberto_itemready,
    spell:       [spr_bberto_spell, "idle", 1],
    spellready:  spr_bberto_spellready,
    victory:     [spr_bberto_victory, true],
    spare:       [spr_bberto_act, "idle", 1],
    attack_eff:  spr_bberto_attackeff,
}

    Remember: every sprite named there must already exist.

    Step 5 — Icons¶

    Three small sprites represent him in the menus. Berto currently uses the engine's placeholders (spr_ui_default_icon and spr_ui_default_head).

    Field Where it appears Size (all base-game ones) Origin
    s_icon The battle box, and the party menu 35 × 24 0, 0
    s_icon_ow Party Select and the overworld menu 25 × 20 0, 0
    s_icon_weapon The equipment menu 13 × 18 0, 5

    For icons, the origin is the top-left, the opposite of the character sprites.

    Create spr_ui_berto_icon (35 × 24), spr_ui_berto_head (25 × 20) and spr_ui_berto_icon_hurt (35 × 24), and set:

    s_icon    = spr_ui_berto_icon
s_icon_ow = spr_ui_berto_head

    Notice the third sprite: when a party member is hurt, the battle box swaps the icon for the one named after it plus _hurt (party_get_icon_hurt, scripts/party_get/party_get.gml). Noelle has spr_ui_noelle_icon and spr_ui_noelle_icon_hurt. You never write the hurt one into the constructor; the engine builds its name, so it must be spelled exactly <icon name>_hurt. I have not tested what happens when it is missing, so create it.

    For s_icon_weapon, the engine ships a few weapon icons (spr_ui_menu_weapon_sword, _axe, _ring). Pick the one that matches his weapon until you draw your own.

    Common problems¶

    Symptom Likely cause
    The game fails when a fight starts A sprite named in battle_sprites does not exist, or is misspelled
    Berto jumps sideways or up when the pose changes The origins of his battle sprites do not match
    He floats above or sinks into the ground The battle sprite's origin is not at the feet
    He shows Susie's pose for an action That entry is not replaced yet (Step 4), or it is misspelled
    The slash effect appears off-centre attack_eff's origin is not its centre
    The battle box shows a grey placeholder icon s_icon is not set, or the sprite is missing

    I have read each of these steps in the engine's code, but have not run a custom character through a full fight in GameMaker. If a step does not behave as written, treat it as a bug in this chapter.
